    Commercial Paver Sealing: Keeping Business Surfaces Professional

    📅 July 2025 ⏱ 5 min read ✍️ Go Go Gloss

    Commercial vs Residential Sealing

    Paver sealing at a commercial property is a different undertaking than a residential job in almost every dimension. The square footage is larger — sometimes dramatically so. The traffic is heavier, with dozens or hundreds of people crossing the surface daily instead of one family. Business continuity constraints mean the work often has to happen at night or over a weekend. And the documentation requirements are more rigorous, with most commercial clients needing written scope, material specifications, and proof of insurance before any work begins.

    The products are also different. Commercial paver applications warrant sealers with higher solids content — the percentage of actual sealer material that remains on the surface after the carrier evaporates. Higher-solids sealers build a thicker protective film, handle repeated foot and vehicle traffic better, and resist the chemical exposures common to commercial environments. What works fine for a residential patio sealing twice a year would wear out within months under commercial traffic.

    Go Go Gloss serves commercial clients across Scottsdale and Metro Phoenix, including retail centers, restaurant groups, hotels, office parks, and medical facilities. Here is what makes commercial paver sealing work in a business context.

    High-Traffic Considerations

    Commercial paver surfaces are not all the same — and the right sealer for each location depends heavily on what that surface has to endure:

    • Retail entries and parking areas need high-durability sealers formulated for vehicle traffic, combined with anti-skid additives. Customer entry points are high foot-traffic zones where a slippery surface creates both safety risk and first-impression problems.
    • Restaurant patios and outdoor dining areas require sealers with strong resistance to food oils, grease, acidic beverages, and cleaning chemicals. The staining risk in a restaurant patio environment is substantially higher than a typical residential patio — and pavers that stain are pavers that look dirty to customers.
    • Hotel pool decks and aquatic areas have two non-negotiable requirements: anti-slip ratings for wet surfaces, and chemical resistance against pool chlorine, algaecides, and pH-adjustment chemicals that splash and drip onto the surrounding paver surface. Standard residential pool deck sealers are not formulated for this environment.
    • Office and medical facility entries typically need a clean, professional finish that holds up to daily traffic while meeting any anti-slip requirements in building codes or ADA standards.

    Liability and Slip Prevention

    Businesses carry significantly greater liability exposure than homeowners when a slip-and-fall incident occurs on their property. A customer who falls on a slippery sealed surface at a retail center or hotel pool deck has legal recourse against the business, and the cost of that exposure far exceeds the cost of proper sealing with appropriate anti-slip treatment.

    The relevant standard for commercial slip resistance is ASTM D2047, which measures the coefficient of friction (COF) of floor surfaces. A wet COF above 0.5 is the general benchmark for pedestrian surfaces — below that, surfaces are considered slip-hazardous in wet conditions. Standard gloss and wet-look sealers applied without anti-slip additives often fall below this threshold when wet, which is why anti-slip additives are not optional on commercial applications.

    Go Go Gloss provides:

    • Anti-slip additives mixed into the sealer for all pool decks, entry areas, and high-traffic walkways as standard practice on commercial jobs
    • Certificate of insurance naming the property owner or manager as additional insured, provided before work begins
    • Written scope of work documenting what was applied, where, and when — creating a maintenance record that protects the business if a liability question arises

    Scheduling Around Business Operations

    The biggest operational challenge in commercial paver sealing is timing. Closing a retail entry or restaurant patio during business hours is not acceptable for most clients — which means the work has to happen when the business is closed or at minimum traffic.

    Our commercial scheduling approach:

    • Night and weekend sealing available — We schedule crews for evening and overnight starts when needed. Most commercial sealers are dry to foot traffic in 2–4 hours at Arizona temperatures, meaning areas sealed at 9 PM are accessible for morning business hours.
    • Phased approach for large surfaces — Rather than closing an entire parking lot or entry complex at once, we seal half the area, allow it to cure, then do the second half. Traffic maintains access to the facility throughout.
    • Vehicle re-entry timing — Vehicles should stay off freshly sealed pavers for 24–48 hours depending on temperature and sealer type. We coordinate with property management to arrange temporary parking solutions where needed.
    • Advance notice to tenants and staff — For multi-tenant commercial properties, we provide written notice with dates, affected areas, and re-entry times for distribution to tenants.

    Written Scope for Management Approval

    Most commercial paver sealing jobs require management or ownership approval before work begins — often as part of a formal procurement process or maintenance budget approval. The documentation we provide is structured to support that process:

    • Itemized quote by zone — Each distinct area (main entry, parking field, loading area, patio) listed separately with square footage and pricing, so management can approve by zone if needed
    • Sealer product data sheets — Technical documentation on the specific sealer product being used, including solids content, slip resistance data, chemical resistance ratings, and recommended maintenance intervals
    • Certificate of insurance and business license — Available upon request for any commercial engagement
    • Timeline and scheduling plan — Written confirmation of work dates, hours, and phasing to allow property management to coordinate around business operations

    Commercial Paver Sealing Pricing

    Commercial pricing reflects the scale, product grade, and scheduling requirements of the job. Key factors:

    • Volume discounts apply for large commercial jobs — the per-square-foot cost decreases as total area increases
    • Night and weekend scheduling carries a modest premium over standard daytime residential rates
    • Higher-solids commercial sealers cost more per gallon than standard residential products but cover more area per gallon and last longer under commercial conditions
    • Maintenance contracts available for ongoing biennial or annual resealing — contract pricing locks in rates and ensures priority scheduling
