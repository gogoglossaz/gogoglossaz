    How Much Does Paver Sealing Cost in Scottsdale?

    📅 July 2025 ⏱ 5 min read ✍️ Go Go Gloss

    Pricing is the question we hear most often, and it's also the hardest to answer without more information — because paver sealing costs vary significantly based on surface size, paver type, current condition, and what's included in the scope of work. This guide breaks down real price ranges in the Scottsdale market, explains what drives costs up or down, and helps you evaluate whether a quote you've received is fair.

    Scottsdale Paver Sealing Price Range

    Here are typical price ranges for common project types in the Scottsdale and Metro Phoenix market in 2025:

    • Small patio (200–500 sq ft): $200–$400. These are quick jobs with lower material costs. Setup and mobilization make smaller jobs slightly higher per sq ft.
    • Average driveway (500–1,000 sq ft): $350–$600. The most common residential project. Includes power washing, inspection, and two-coat application.
    • Large driveway/patio combo (1,000–2,000 sq ft): $600–$1,200. Larger areas achieve more efficient sq ft pricing. Prep time remains a significant portion of cost.
    • Pool deck (varies widely): $300–$800. Pool decks vary dramatically in size and complexity. Travertine pool decks with specialty anti-slip sealer fall at the higher end.

    These ranges assume surfaces in reasonable condition requiring standard preparation. Jobs with significant issues — old sealer to strip, heavy staining, efflorescence, or damaged joint sand — add to the base price.

    Price Per Square Foot

    The most useful way to compare quotes is on a per-square-foot basis. In the Scottsdale market, professional paver sealing typically runs $0.35–$0.75 per sq ft for standard work:

    • $0.35–$0.45/sq ft: Large, flat concrete paver jobs in good condition. Simple surfaces with easy access and minimal prep.
    • $0.45–$0.60/sq ft: Most standard residential jobs — average driveways, patios, walkways with normal prep requirements.
    • $0.60–$0.75/sq ft: Travertine surfaces requiring specialty penetrating sealer, pool decks with anti-slip requirements, or jobs requiring extra prep time.
    • Above $0.75/sq ft: Jobs with significant additional scope — old sealer stripping, heavy efflorescence treatment, full joint sand replacement.

    If a quote comes in significantly below $0.35/sq ft for a standard job, ask detailed questions about what's included and what sealer product will be used. Unusually low pricing usually reflects either consumer-grade materials or preparation shortcuts.

    What's Included in a Professional Quote

    A complete professional paver sealing job should include the following without additional charges:

    • Power washing: A thorough pressure wash to remove dirt, algae, and surface debris — typically at 2,500–3,500 PSI for concrete pavers.
    • Joint sand inspection: Checking whether polymeric joint sand is intact or needs topping off. Minor sand top-offs are often included; full replacement is usually a line-item add-on.
    • Two-coat sealer application: A quality job applies two thin, even coats with appropriate dry time between them.
    • Surface protection and cleanup: Masking off adjacent surfaces, protecting landscaping, and cleaning up equipment and overspray.

    Some companies charge separately for re-sanding joints with polymeric sand, which is a reasonable and common add-on. Expect $50–$150 extra depending on the amount of sand needed and the area size. Ask upfront whether this is included or separate.

    What Drives the Price Up

    Several factors legitimately increase the cost of a paver sealing job beyond the base price:

    • Travertine vs concrete pavers: Travertine requires specialty penetrating sealers that cost more per gallon and are applied differently. Expect 15–25% higher pricing compared to equivalent concrete paver jobs.
    • Old sealer stripping: If your pavers have an existing sealer that has degraded, delaminated, or is incompatible with the new product, it must be chemically stripped before resealing. This adds $150–$300+ depending on the area and severity, and it's not optional — sealing over failed sealer almost always fails quickly.
    • Heavily stained surfaces: Oil stains, rust, paint, or heavy efflorescence requires extra cleaning time and sometimes specialty chemicals before sealing. Most contractors price this as an hourly add-on once scope is assessed on-site.
    • Difficult access: Properties in gated communities with tight access, jobs requiring equipment to be carried through a home, or alley driveways that complicate pressure washing setup can add to labor cost.
    • Anti-slip additives: Required for pool decks and often added to sloped driveways. Adds $50–$100 to a typical job.

    Red Flags in Quotes

    Not all quotes are equal. Watch for these warning signs when evaluating bids:

    • Unusually low total price: A $150 quote for a 1,000 sq ft driveway in Scottsdale is not a deal — it's a signal that the job will involve consumer-grade materials, minimal preparation, or both. Low-price providers often use high-dilution sealers that look fine for 3–6 months then fail completely.
    • No mention of power washing: Sealing without thorough cleaning is almost guaranteed to produce poor results. Any professional quote should explicitly include a pressure wash.
    • Per-gallon pricing with no sq ft estimate: Pricing by the gallon rather than by area makes it impossible to compare quotes and can lead to upcharges once work starts. Always get a flat price tied to a specific square footage.
    • No specification of the sealer product: A reputable company should be able to tell you the brand and product name of the sealer they'll apply. "Professional-grade sealer" without specifics is a yellow flag.
    • No written contract: Verbal quotes with no written scope of work create disputes. Get everything in writing before work begins.

    The Scottsdale market has reputable contractors at fair prices. Getting two or three quotes from established companies — checking reviews and verifying they specify commercial-grade products — will give you a solid baseline for what a fair price looks like for your specific project.
