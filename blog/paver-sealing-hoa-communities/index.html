Paver Sealing for HOA Communities: A Complete Guide

📅 July 2025 ⏱ 6 min read ✍️ Go Go Gloss

Why HOA Paver Maintenance Is Different

Paver sealing for a homeowners association is a fundamentally different undertaking than a residential job. The scale alone changes everything — a single HOA community might have thousands of square feet of paved surface spread across entry monuments, common area walkways, parking areas, pool decks, and individual unit driveways. Each of those surfaces has different traffic levels, different sealer needs, and different scheduling constraints.

Beyond scale, HOA work involves multiple decision-makers. Most boards require formal bids, written scopes, insurance documentation, and a vote before any significant maintenance expenditure is approved. Community managers often coordinate between the board, the contractor, and residents simultaneously. And once the work is approved, the contractor must work around the lives of dozens or hundreds of households — not just one family's schedule.

Go Go Gloss has worked with HOAs and property managers across Scottsdale and Metro Phoenix. This guide covers what makes community-scale paver sealing work smoothly — from the bid process through project completion.

Written Quotes for Board Approval

Most HOA boards cannot approve a maintenance expenditure based on a verbal estimate and a handshake. They need documentation that protects the association legally, justifies the budget line, and gives them something to reference if questions arise later.

A proper HOA paver sealing quote should include:

  • Itemized scope by location — Main entry, parking areas, pool deck, common walkways, and any other zones listed separately with square footage and per-zone pricing
  • Material specifications — Sealer brand and product name, solids content, number of coats, and application method
  • Prep work included — What cleaning, efflorescence treatment, and joint sand work is included in the base price vs. priced as an add-on
  • Timeline — Estimated start date, duration per phase, and re-entry times for residents
  • Weather contingency — What happens if sealing is scheduled and monsoon weather arrives (most professional contractors build in a reschedule policy)
  • Certificate of insurance — General liability and workers' compensation documentation, required by most HOAs before work begins

Go Go Gloss provides fully itemized written quotes structured for board presentation. We can also attend board meetings or provide supplemental documentation if the approval process requires it.

Scheduling Large-Scale Community Jobs

Coordination is where community paver jobs succeed or fail. A poorly planned schedule creates resident complaints, access conflicts, and quality problems that are difficult to resolve after the fact.

Our standard approach for HOA scheduling:

  • Phase by location, not all at once — Main entry and community roads are never sealed simultaneously; we sequence zones so access is maintained at all times
  • Resident notice 24–48 hours in advance — Written notice distributed by the HOA manager to all affected residents with the date, affected areas, and re-entry time
  • Overnight sealing for parking areas — Parking lots and driveways can often be sealed after 9 PM and re-opened to vehicles by 6 AM the following morning, minimizing disruption
  • Re-entry timing — Foot traffic is typically safe in 2–4 hours; vehicles should stay off sealed pavers for 24–48 hours depending on temperature and sealer type

For communities with active pool use or gated entry systems, we schedule those areas last and work with the property manager to arrange temporary access solutions during the curing window.

Maintenance Contracts

HOAs that manage paver maintenance reactively — waiting until surfaces look bad before calling for bids — consistently pay more and get worse outcomes than those on a planned maintenance schedule.

Go Go Gloss offers annual and biennial maintenance contracts for HOA communities that include:

  • Locked-in pricing — Contract pricing protects the HOA budget from material cost increases between seal cycles
  • Priority scheduling — Contract clients are scheduled first during the high-demand winter season (October–March), when conditions are ideal and contractor availability is tightest
  • Condition monitoring — We flag areas showing early wear at each visit, allowing the board to address spot issues before they require full replacement work
  • Simplified approval process — Annual maintenance under an existing contract often requires only board ratification rather than a full new-bid process

Common HOA Locations We Seal

Across the HOA communities we serve in Scottsdale, Paradise Valley, Gilbert, and Chandler, the most common paver sealing locations include:

  • Community entries and monument areas — The first impression of the community; high visibility and often the most detailed paver work
  • Pool decks and recreation areas — Anti-slip additives required; chemical resistance from pool water and chlorine splash important
  • Parking areas and fire lanes — High vehicle load; solvent-based sealers with high solids content recommended
  • Common walkways and sidewalks — High foot traffic; consistent appearance across the community important for HOA standards
  • Unit driveways (where HOA maintains common driveways) — Individual unit scheduling coordinated through the property manager

Liability and Slip Prevention

HOAs carry greater liability exposure than individual homeowners when it comes to slip-and-fall incidents on common area surfaces. A sealed paver that becomes slippery when wet — particularly around pools, entry areas, and high-foot-traffic walkways — creates real risk for the association.

Our standard for any HOA pool deck or common area sealing:

  • Anti-slip additives mixed into the sealer for all pool decks, spa areas, and high-traffic entry paths — this is not optional for common areas
  • Certificate of insurance provided before any work begins, naming the HOA as an additional insured where required
  • Written scope of work that documents exactly what was applied, where, and when — this record protects the HOA if a liability question arises after the project

If your HOA has had a slip incident or received a complaint about a slippery surface, we can assess the area and apply a retrofit anti-slip treatment without requiring a full reseal.
